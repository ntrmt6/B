Understanding the Technology: How Do They Actually Work?

Before purchasing, it is critical to understand how different devices take their measurements. The accuracy of your readings heavily depends on the underlying technology.

The Golden Rule: Smartwatches with physical inflating cuffs (Oscillometric) are significantly more accurate for standalone readings than those using light sensors (PPG).

1. Oscillometric Method (Miniature Airbags)

This is the gold standard for wrist-based blood pressure measurement. Smartwatches using this technology (like the YHE BP Doctor Fit or Huawei Watch D2) feature a physical, ultra-thin mechanical airbag integrated into the watch strap. When you initiate a reading, the airbag inflates, temporarily restricting blood flow, exactly like a traditional arm cuff. Sensors detect the pressure changes as your pulse beats against the cuff to determine your systolic and diastolic pressure.

2. Photoplethysmography (PPG) / Optical Sensors

Most mainstream smartwatches (like the Samsung Galaxy Watch 8) use PPG sensors. These shine a light into your skin to track the volume of blood pulsing through your capillaries. AI algorithms then estimate your blood pressure based on pulse wave analysis. Crucial warning: Optical sensors do not measure direct pressure. They require regular calibration (usually every 28 days) with a standard upper-arm cuff to maintain accuracy. They are excellent for tracking trends, but cannot replace a medical monitor.
